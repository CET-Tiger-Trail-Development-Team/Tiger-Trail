2. The SHED (Student Hall for Exploration and Development)
Filters: NAVIGATION, LORE

Opened fall 2023. $120 million building physically connected to Wallace Library — the campus's hub for technology, art, and design.

Active Classrooms — extra-large, flexible spaces built for group work, not lectures
Makerspaces — seven of them (3D printing, woodworking, textiles, electronics); open to all majors, not just engineering
Project Teams — seven competitive student teams are based here (e-vehicle, robotics, rocket, etc.)
The Arts — Sklarsky Glass Box Theater (185-seat flexible performance space), plus dance and music studios

Link: https://www.rit.edu/shed


3. The Co-op Requirement
Filters: MCET, SURVIVAL

Cooperative Education (co-op) is a strict graduation requirement for most STEM and business majors, including MCET. Full-time (35+ hrs/week), paid, major-relevant work — this is why programs like Computer Science or Mechanical Engineering take five years. No RIT tuition during a co-op block, but housing/living costs are the student's responsibility.

The four-step cycle:

Complete Prerequisites (first two years) — 2.0+ GPA (typical minimum), first two years of coursework, department-specific Co-op Prep Seminar (resume + interview practice)
Search and Interview (1–2 semesters before working) — RIT's Fall/Spring Career Fairs, applying via Career Connect and LinkedIn; international students coordinate CPT authorization with International Student Services
Accept and Report — report the job in Career Connect, register the co-op block in SIS
Work and Evaluate (12–15 weeks) — full-time work, employer evaluation near term end, Student Work Report submitted to the department for an "S" (Satisfactory) grade

Links:

Career Services & Co-op: https://www.rit.edu/careerservices/
Career Fairs: https://www.rit.edu/careerservices/students/career-fairs
International Student Services: https://www.rit.edu/studentaffairs/iss/


4. Campus Life & Geography
Filters: NAVIGATION, LORE
The Quarter Mile and the Tunnels
Main pedestrian walkway linking east-side dorms to west-side academic buildings. Two separate tunnel networks handle Rochester winters: one connects dorms (also houses the post office, laundry, convenience stores), the other connects academic buildings.
Special Interest Houses (SIHs)
Themed living communities on specific residence hall floors (or the Greek quad) — skip the standard dorm lottery, interview required. Options: Computer Science House (CSH), Engineering House, House of the Arts, House of General Science, Unity House, Photo House, Douglass Leadership House & Drama House. Link: https://www.rit.edu/housing/special-interest-houses
NTID and Deaf Culture
RIT hosts the National Technical Institute for the Deaf (NTID). Deaf and hard-of-hearing peers are in your classes; ASL functions as the campus's unofficial second language, with interpreters and real-time captioning common in lectures. An intro ASL elective is a widely recommended way to connect with the community. Link: https://www.rit.edu/ntid/
Getting Off Campus
No car: free shuttles loop to Wegmans, Target, and Marketplace Mall (all in Henrietta)
Downtown Rochester (South Wedge, Park Avenue, Public Market): generally need a car or Uber — shuttles don't reach that far


5. RIT Housing
Filters: SURVIVAL, NAVIGATION

Structure note: top-level page titled "RIT Housing," linking out to subsections below (Freshman/First-Year, Upperclassman RIT-Owned, Off-Campus Private, General Off-Campus). Each subsection carries its own detail and resource links.
Co-op + Housing Interaction (governs all RIT-owned housing)
Prerequisite for any accommodation: your co-op must be more than 45 miles from campus. Local or virtual co-ops require maintaining your normal housing contract.

Leaving for a Fall co-op — two options:

Hold your space: pay 50% of normal Fall rent, belongings stay in the room, key access deactivated until Spring, guaranteed same room/roommates
Cancel for free: co-op is a valid exemption from cancellation fees, full move-out required, RIT guarantees housing (not the same room) for Spring return

Leaving for a Spring co-op — no hold option. Full check-out and key return at end of Fall semester. As long as the co-op is registered in SIS, Spring rent is fully waived with no termination fees.
Freshman / First-Year Housing
Generally required: RIT-owned housing + campus meal plan.

Residence Halls: high-rises (Ellingson, Gleason, Peterson) and low-rises; mostly traditional doubles with communal bathrooms, a few suite-style buildings
SIHs: see above
RIT Inn & Conference Center: ~10 min drive off campus, used for overflow — larger rooms, private bathrooms, dedicated shuttle
Upperclassman Housing (RIT-Owned)
Available via housing lottery after year one. Because RIT owns these, co-op contract breaks/pauses are free (see co-op interaction above).

Meal Plans by Year/Housing
First-years in dorms: required — Roar or Roar Plus (set number of Gracie's Meals, e.g. 60–100/semester, plus $2,100+ in Dining Dollars)

Convenience Stores (Corner Store, Sol's) — near dorms/tunnels, campus-bodega basics plus toiletries and cold medicine
Coffee Shops — Beanz (base of Quarter Mile, Finger Lakes Coffee Roasters), Artesano Bakery (pastries), Midnight Oil (near the SHED, late-night study spot)

7. Dietary Accommodations
Filters: SURVIVAL

RIT ranks among the top allergy-friendly colleges nationally (Spokin Top 100).
Severe Allergies
Registered Dietitian — full-time RIT Dining staff; email diningRD@rit.edu for a 1:1 consult, dining hall walkthrough, and chef introductions
Gracie's is nut-free — peanut/tree-nut-free except coconut
"Simply Eats" station (inside Gracie's) — free of 8 of the top 9 allergens (peanuts, tree nuts, wheat/gluten, eggs, milk, shellfish, fish, sesame — note: soy is still used); staff wear purple coats/use purple utensils
Disability Services Office (DSO) — for allergies too severe to manage safely in dining halls; can grant reduced meal plans or access to a private kitchen (e.g., Baker Hall)
Vegan / Vegetarian
2026: RIT became the first university in New York State with a 50% plant-based menu across all dining locations
Extensive options everywhere (plant-based brick-oven pizza, tofu scrambles, meat-substitute grill items)
Digital menus labeled with green V (vegetarian) / VE (vegan)
Halal and Kosher
Kosher: Star-K certified; dedicated Kosher deli/restaurant on campus; pre-packaged Kosher items in convenience stores
Halal: not a fully Halal-certified campus, but poultry (breasts, legs, quarters) is certified Halal — antibiotic-free, 100% vegetarian diet, USA-harvested
Many Muslim students eat at the Kosher deli since Kosher slaughter rules overlap heavily with Halal requirements
Tech Tools
NetNutrition and FD MealPlanner app — filter menus by allergen (e.g., dairy + gluten-free) before leaving the dorm; also shows full macro breakdowns
Link: https://www.rit.edu/dining/nutrition
